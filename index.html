The 4 formulas

“Percent” simply means “out of 100”. Every percentage question is one of these four.

X% of Y

Result = Y × X ÷ 100

Multiply the number by the percent, then divide by 100. Example: 20% of 500 = 500 × 20 ÷ 100 = 100.

X is what % of Y

Percent = X ÷ Y × 100

Divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100. Example: 45 ÷ 60 × 100 = 75%.

% increase / decrease

Change % = (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100

Find the difference, divide by the starting (old) value. Positive = increase, negative = decrease.

Discount price

You pay = Price − (Price × % off ÷ 100)

First find the discount amount, then subtract it from the original price.

Common questions

How do I find a percentage of a number quickly in my head?

Find 10% first (move the decimal one place left — 10% of 850 is 85). Then build up: 5% is half of 10% (42.5), 20% is double 10% (170), 1% is the decimal moved two places (8.5). Combine them — 15% of 850 = 85 + 42.5 = 127.5.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

If an interest rate rises from 8% to 10%, that is a rise of 2 percentage points — but a 25% increase in the rate itself (2 ÷ 8 × 100). News reports often mix the two up.

Can a percentage be more than 100%?

Yes. 150% of 200 is 300 — more than the original. Profit, growth and “extra” amounts regularly go above 100%. A decrease, however, can never be more than 100% (you can't lose more than everything).

How do I reverse a percentage — e.g. price includes 18% GST?

Divide by (1 + rate/100). If the total is ₹1,180 including 18% GST, the base price is 1180 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1,000 and the GST part is ₹180. Don't subtract 18% of 1,180 — that gives the wrong answer.

Why is a 50% loss not recovered by a 50% gain?

Because the base changes. ₹100 falls 50% → ₹50. Then ₹50 rising 50% adds only ₹25 → ₹75. To get back to ₹100 you need a 100% gain on ₹50. Try it in the Increase / Decrease mode above.
